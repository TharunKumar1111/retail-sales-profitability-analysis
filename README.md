# Retail Sales Performance & Profitability Analysis
**Excel | SQL | Power BI** - an end-to-end data analytics portfolio project

> **One-line summary:** Sales grew 48% in two years but margin stayed flat at ~14%. This project finds where the profit leaks: discounts above 20% turn orders loss-making, and two sub-categories (Tables, Machines) plus one region (Central) drag margin down.

![Excel dashboard](images/excel_dashboard-1.png)

## 1. Business problem
A multi-category Indian retailer wants to understand why growing sales are not producing growing profit. Full brief: [`docs/Business_Problem_Document.pdf`](docs/Business_Problem_Document.pdf).

## 2. Dataset
* **Synthetic** order-line data (generated by `gen_data.py`, fixed seed) with realistic problems: duplicates, text dates, inconsistent casing, missing values, impossible values.
* 8,996 raw rows -> **8,939 clean rows**, 4,295 orders, 832 customers, 65 products, 20 states, Jan 2023 - Dec 2025, INR.

## 5. Method
1. **Clean** - 7 issues found and fixed; every step logged in `data/cleaning_log.csv` and the Excel `Cleaning_Log` sheet.
2. **Model** - flat file split into `customers`, `products`, `orders`, `order_lines`; staging-vs-fact reconciliation matches to the rupee.
3. **Analyse** - the same KPIs computed independently in Excel and SQL; both agree exactly (Sales INR 53,583,538; Profit INR 7,417,634).
4. **Visualise** - Power BI dashboard on the star schema.

## 6. Key findings
| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales grew +25% (2024) and +18% (2025) but margin stayed ~13.6-14.4% | Excel Summary 1, SQL Q2 |
| 2 | **Discounts above 20% destroy profit.** Margin: 27% (no discount) -> 19% (1-10%) -> 9% (11-20%) -> **-10% (21-30%)** -> **-37% (>30%)**. 79% of lines with >30% discount lose money | SQL Q6 |
| 3 | **Tables (-5.3% margin) and Machines (-3.1%) are loss-making**; both average ~21-22% discount | SQL Q4, Q7 |
| 4 | Office Supplies earn 24% margin on 23% of sales; Furniture only 8.6% margin on 44% of sales | Excel Summary 2 |
| 5 | **Central region** has the lowest margin (12.0%), deepest discounts (13.2% vs ~10%), most loss-making lines (12.2% vs ~8%) and slowest Standard delivery (7.0 days, 41% > 7 days) | SQL Q5, Q10 |
| 6 | Festive peak: Oct-Dec index 121-137 vs 100 average | SQL Q12 |
| 7 | 172 "Champion" customers = INR 33.6M of INR 53.6M sales (63%); 102 high-value customers are "at risk" (avg 408 days since last order) | SQL Q9 |
| 8 | Top 10 customers = 21% of profit; repeat rate is 81% | SQL Q8, Q14 |

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Cap discounts at 20%** (approval workflow above that). Re-pricing the 1,284 lines discounted >20% to a 20% cap would turn their combined profit from INR -1.84M to INR +0.40M, about +INR 2.2M (+30% of total profit), *assuming volumes do not change* - a best case that should be tested with a pilot.
2. **Reprice or delist Tables and Machines** (worst products: Ultra Table E10, Prime Machine B62, Eco Table D9).
3. **Fix Central region**: audit discount approvals and review the logistics partner.
4. **Plan for Oct-Dec**: build stock early, but pre-set discount limits so the peak does not erode margin.
5. **Win-back campaign** for the 102 at-risk high-value customers; **loyalty** for Champions.

## 9. Limitations
Synthetic data (findings demonstrate method, not a real company). No returns, costs by overhead or customer acquisition data. The discount-cap estimate holds volume constant and ignores price elasticity.
